# ***What Is The Difference Between A Skewer And A Pin?***

>*A skewer is very similar to a [pin](https://www.chess.com/terms/pin-chess), but there is one decisive difference. In a skewer, the most valuable piece is the one suffering a direct attack, while a weaker piece stands behind it. In a pin, the opposite happens.*

>*The image below shows both tactics being employed. On the left, you can see that the queen is being attacked first by the rook on b1. Since it is the more valuable piece of the attacked pair, it will move to save itself, allowing the rook to take the bishop on b8. On the right, the opposite happens. The bishop is attacked by the rook on g1, but it cannot move since that would expose the king on g8.*
![[-- IMG -- What Is The Difference Between A Skewer And A Pin.png|300]]

*Basically the difference is in the order of the pieces:*
**Skewer**: The piece with a higher value is moved, so you can take the one of less value
**Pin**: *The oppossite* If opponent move the piece that you are threatening, then he would lose a piece with a higher value (or a check). In other words, that piece that you are threatening and all his moves that could have done, are disabled because of your pin.

*(IN WORDS OF CHESS.COM):*
>In a pin, the attacked piece cannot move because it prevents a more significant threat. On the other hand, a skewer works in reverse. The most valuable piece is upfront, so it must move and allow the capture of the weaker piece behind it.
